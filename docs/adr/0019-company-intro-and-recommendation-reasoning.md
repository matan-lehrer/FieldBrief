# 0019. Plain-English company intro, recommendations with a reasoning chain

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-23

## Context
Two presentation gaps from Matan's quality-pass spec. First: the report jumped straight from the company name into the executive brief and frameworks — a reader unfamiliar with the company had to reconstruct "what does this company even do" from scattered snapshot fields before any strategic finding made sense. Second: `Recommendation` was just `statement` + `rationale` + `priority` — useful as a one-liner, but it couldn't show *why* it should be trusted (what it assumes, what would disprove it), which is exactly what the Synamedia regression showed was missing: a recommendation that sounded authoritative but rested on a single announcement with no stated assumption or way to check it.

## Decision
- `FrameworkSynthesisResult` gains `company_introductions`: one 2-3 sentence plain-English paragraph per company (what it sells, who buys it, its market), grounded in claim_ids like every other section, generated by the *same* existing single synthesis call — no new LLM call, marginal token cost only. Rendered in both templates right after the company name/meta, before the executive brief, matching "readers should understand the business before seeing strategic conclusions." Omitted per-company when unsupported, same discipline as every other section (never invent one to fill the slot).
- `Recommendation` (and `RecommendationDraft`) gain `action` (the concrete thing to actually do or investigate), `expected_benefit` (what it's meant to achieve), `key_assumption` (the single biggest thing it assumes true), and `next_validation_step` (how to check that assumption before committing real resources). The synthesis prompt requires all four; the empty-recommendations state changed from the uninformative "No recommendations yet" to an explicit "insufficient evidence to recommend action with confidence."

## Consequences
- A reader gets oriented before being asked to evaluate strategic claims, in both HTML and PDF.
- Every recommendation now states its own falsifiable assumption and a concrete next step — closer to how a real strategy memo would present a recommendation, and harder to read as more certain than the evidence supports.
- Cost: one more required field set on the existing single synthesis call; no new call type, no new loop.
- When we would revisit this: if `next_validation_step` responses turn out formulaic/generic in real runs (a risk with any new required LLM field), tighten the prompt's guidance rather than add a fifth required field.
